Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, 2011
Extracted key rules for compliance rule-engine development — SIH26034 | Source: Gazette of India, GSR
202(E), 7 March 2011
1. Key Definitions (Rule 2)
Manufacturer: A person/firm who produces, makes or manufactures the commodity, or puts a mark claiming it was
made by them.
Packer: A person/firm who pre-packs any commodity in a bottle, tin, wrapper or otherwise, in units suitable for sale.
Net quantity: The quantity by weight, measure or number of commodity contained in a package, EXCLUDING the
packaging/wrapper.
Retail sale price (MRP): The maximum price at which the commodity may be sold to the consumer. Must be printed
as: 'Maximum or Max. retail price Rs.../inclusive of all taxes' or 'MRP Rs.../incl. of all taxes'.
Retail package: Packages intended for retail sale to the ultimate consumer. Does NOT include industrial or
institutional consumers.
Principal display panel: The surface area of the package where all required declarations are grouped and displayed.
2. Applicability (Rule 3)
Chapter II (retail package rules) does NOT apply to: (a) packages containing more than 25 kg or 25 litre (except
cement/fertilizer sold in bags up to 50 kg); (b) packages meant for industrial or institutional consumers (e.g. hotels,
hospitals, airlines buying directly from manufacturer for own use).
3. Mandatory Declarations on Every Package (Rule 6)
Every retail package must bear the following declarations, clearly and conspicuously:
# Field Requirement
a Manufacturer/Packer/
Importer name & address
Name and complete address required. If manufacturer ≠ packer, both names/addresses needed. For imported packages, importer's name/address also needed. EXCEPTION: for food articles, this rule is replaced by Prevention of Food Adulteration Act, 1954 requirements instead.
b Common/generic name Plain name of the commodity. If package has more than one product, each product's name and quantity must be mentioned.
c Net quantity In standard unit of weight/measure, or number if sold by count.
d Month & year of
manufacture/packing/import
Required, with some exceptions (bidis, incense sticks, certain LPG cylinders don't need this). For food articles, governed by Prevention of Food Adulteration Act instead.
e Retail sale price (MRP) Must show 'Maximum/Max retail price Rs..../inclusive of all taxes' format. Exception: bidis and certain LPG cylinders don't need this declared.
f Dimensions Required only where size of the commodity is relevant (e.g. bedsheets, fabric).
g Consumer care details Name, address, telephone number, and email (if available) of contact for consumer complaints — required on every package (Rule 6(2)).
Important sub-rules: No individual stickers allowed to alter/make declarations (except MRP reduction stickers). Where a
name/address appears without 'manufactured by' or 'packed by' qualifier, it is presumed to be the manufacturer. If a brand owner's
name/address appears as marketer, the brand owner is held responsible for violations.

4. Principal Display Panel — Font Size Rules (Rule 7)
The height of letters in any declaration shall NOT be less than 1mm (normal case), or 2mm when blown, formed,
molded, embossed or perforated on the container. Width of letter/numeral shall not be less than one-third of its height
(except numeral '1' and letters i, I, l).
Table I — Minimum numeral height by net quantity (weight/volume):
Net Quantity Normal Case (mm) Blown/Embossed (mm)
Up to 200g/ml 1 2
Above 200g/ml up to 500g/ml 2 4
Above 500g/ml 4 6
Table II — Minimum numeral height by net quantity (length/area/number):

Above 2500 cm2 6 6
5. Manner of Declaration (Rule 9)
• Every declaration must be legible and prominent.
• Numerals of retail sale price and net quantity must be printed/painted/inscribed in a colour that CONTRASTS with the
label background.
• Exception: if label info is blown/formed/molded on glass or plastic, contrasting colour is not required.
• Hand-written/hand-script declarations must be clear, unambiguous and legible.
• No declaration shall require reading through any liquid commodity in the package.
• Declarations must be in Hindi (Devanagari script) or English (other languages may be added in addition).
6. Declaration Placement (Rule 8)
All declarations must appear on the principal display panel. The area surrounding the quantity declaration must be free
from other printed information — clear space above/below equal to at least the height of the numeral, and clear space
left/right equal to at least twice the height of the numeral.

7. Quantity Declaration Rules (Rules 11-13)
• Net quantity excludes weight of wrappers/packaging materials (Rule 11(1)).
• Quantity declaration must NOT use vague/misleading words: 'minimum', 'not less than', 'average', 'about',
'approximately' or similar (Rule 12(6)).
• No 'dozen', 'score', 'gross', 'great gross' style counting allowed on packages (Rule 13(4)).
• Only SI (International System) units allowed for net quantity (Rule 13(5)).
• Items sold by number must use symbol 'N' or 'U'.
• Units: below 1kg use grams; below 1 litre use millilitres; below 1 metre use centimetres, etc. (Rule 13(2)).

9. 'When Packed' Exemption (Third Schedule)
The following commodities may qualify their net quantity declaration with 'when packed' (accounting for natural weight
loss over time): (1) All kinds of soaps, (2) Lotions, (3) Cream (other than cream of milk).
10. Food Article Exception
For packages containing food articles: manufacturer/packer name-address declaration (Rule 6(1)(a)) and month/year
of manufacture (Rule 6(1)(d)) are governed instead by the Prevention of Food Adulteration Act, 1954 and its rules,
NOT by this Rule directly. Net quantity, MRP, and consumer care rules still apply as normal. This is relevant for testing
on your Lays packet sample.
11. Maximum Permissible Error (First Schedule)
Packages are allowed a small margin of error between declared and actual net quantity. Examples: up to 50g/ml allows
9% error; 100-200g/ml allows 4.5% error; 500-1000g/ml allows 15g/ml error; above 15000g/ml allows 1.0% error. This
is mainly relevant for physical weight-testing, not label/OCR compliance checking.
